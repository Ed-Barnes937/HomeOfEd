sounds/*.wav
  Synthesized in this repo - original work, no third-party audio, no
  attribution owed. Real CC0-sourced one-shots were the first choice (per the
  ticket's sourcing guidance) but weren't reachable from this environment:

    - freesound.org: search/browse works without auth, but every sound's
      actual audio file (including CC0-licensed ones, e.g.
      people/deadrobotmusic/sounds/737300 "Lil Lofi Snare 9", CC0) sits behind
      a login-gated download endpoint - confirmed by fetching the download URL
      directly, which returns the HTML login page rather than audio data, no
      API token available in this environment.
    - opengameart.org "CC0 Sound Effects": browsed the pack; no kick / snare /
      hi-hat / tom / marimba-ish / synth-hit content in it - mostly retro UI
      bleeps, creature and ambience sounds.
    - kenney.nl audio packs: CC0 and directly downloadable, but built for UI /
      sci-fi / RPG sound effects, not tuned percussive or pitched one-shots -
      using them would misrepresent the instruments in the design brief.

  All twenty voices share the same per-voice budget: normalised to peak 0.5
  (the shaker deliberately sits under that, per the spec's "quieter than the
  hat") and under 400 ms, no long tails. Verified by
  apps/boop/src/engine/kitLevels.test.ts - duration, per-voice peak, the
  200 bpm 16th-note retrigger check, and the dense-hit sums (the classic six
  land at ~1.81 raw, the whole roster at ~2.97; the roster figure is what
  ticket 08 sizes MASTER_GAIN against).

  The classic six (ticket 18):

    kick    - sine pitch sweep 150→48 Hz + a 20ms noise click for attack.
    snare   - noise body + two short tonal snaps (330→210 Hz, 200→170 Hz).
    hat     - three inharmonic high partials + a touch of high-passed noise,
              for a metallic tick rather than plain hiss.
    tom     - sine pitch sweep 200→85 Hz, longer and lower than the kick.
    marimba - fundamental (C5, 523.25 Hz) + a bright overtone + a 15ms click
              for mallet attack.
    boop    - two slightly detuned sine sweeps (660→520 Hz, 666→524 Hz) for a
              chorused, electronic, cheeky character.

  The fourteen new voices (ticket 01), rendered by the committed generator
  apps/boop/scripts/generatePlaceholderSamples.mjs:

    Drums
      clap      - three staggered high-passed noise bursts (0 / 9 / 18 ms) over
                  a short noise body: one seed re-struck, 909-style.
      shaker    - a soft high-passed noise pair (0 / 30 ms), scaled to 0.55 of
                  the per-voice peak so it sits under the hat.
      cowbell   - two inharmonic mid partials (540 Hz + 800 Hz), fast decay.
      woodblock - a resonant 1.15 kHz tick with a 4 ms noise click transient.
      triangle  - a high inharmonic stack (4.2 / 5.75 / 7.3 kHz), shimmery,
                  260 ms.
      cymbal    - bright high-passed noise across 390 ms, the longest tail the
                  400 ms cap allows (spec §10.3). Its decay is set by the
                  retrigger rule, not the length: slower tails measured 1.74x
                  at 200 bpm, because even incoherent noise sums in power over
                  eight overlapping hits.

    Notes
      bass      - a ~90 Hz sine pluck (96→88 Hz) with a quiet 180 Hz snap: the
                  low anchor the kit lacked.
      bell      - glockenspiel-ish: C6 (1046.5 Hz) + an inharmonic 2.89 kHz
                  partial, above the marimba's register.
      chime     - three stacked partials (1.57 / 2.35 / 3.14 kHz) under a 30 ms
                  raised-cosine attack, so it arrives softly.
      pluck     - harmonics 330 / 660 / 990 / 1320 Hz with staggered decays, so
                  the tone brightens then dulls like a plucked string.

    Silly
      boing     - a springy vibrato voice (320→150 Hz glide, 17 Hz wobble at
                  30% depth) - the generator's new vibrato block.
      pop       - a 40 ms upward blip (380→1100 Hz), bubble-wrap.
      zap       - a 2 kHz→200 Hz laser sweep in 140 ms.
      drip      - an upward water-drop chirp (620→1700 Hz) with a splash tick.

  No third-party audio. The generator's `voices` map holds all twenty
  definitions, but a bare run rebuilds only the fourteen: the six on disk came
  from ticket 18's generator, which was never committed, so the six definitions
  in the script are ticket 12's originals and no longer match those files
  sample-for-sample. Their recipes above are the record of what shipped.
